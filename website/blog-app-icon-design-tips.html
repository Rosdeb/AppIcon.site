5 app icon design tips that make your app stand out

June 2026 · 4 min read · App Icon Studio

Your app icon is the first thing a potential user sees in the App Store or on a Google Play search result. Before they read the name, the description, or even glance at a screenshot — they see the icon. A great icon earns a tap. A forgettable one gets scrolled past.

Here are five principles used by designers at the world's most-downloaded apps to make icons that are immediately recognizable, even at tiny sizes.

1

Use one strong focal point

The best app icons communicate one idea instantly. Spotify has the speaker waves. Instagram has the camera. Twitter/X has the bird. Ask yourself: if someone saw your icon for half a second, what would they remember? If the answer is "nothing specific," simplify. Remove every element that doesn't reinforce that single focal point. An icon is not a logo presentation — it's a signal.

2

Make it legible at 60px

Design your icon at 1024×1024 but always test it at the size it actually appears on a home screen — around 60×60 points (120×120 pixels on a 2x screen). Complex gradients, fine details, and small text become muddy blobs at this size. Hold your phone at arm's length and squint. If the shape and color still read clearly, you're good. If it turns into a vague smear, simplify the shapes and increase the contrast between the foreground and background.

3

Choose a distinctive, ownable color

Color is the fastest way for returning users to find your app among dozens on a crowded home screen. Pick a color that (a) reflects your brand, (b) isn't already used by a competing app in your category, and (c) provides strong contrast with your foreground element. Blue is the most common app icon color — so if you're in a crowded blue-heavy category like social or productivity, consider whether red, orange, green, or a deep purple would make you stand out more.

4

Avoid text (almost always)

Text in icons almost never works. At home-screen sizes, anything under 3–4 characters becomes unreadable. Even readable text adds visual noise that competes with the icon's core graphic. The only exceptions are apps whose entire brand identity is a single letter or two-letter monogram — like Notion, Figma, or Medium — where the letterform itself is the logo. If you think you need text to explain what the icon is, the icon graphic isn't strong enough. Strengthen the visual instead.

5

Use generous padding and safe zones

Icons that bleed edge-to-edge inside the rounded-square container look cramped and amateurish. Leave breathing room — typically 10–20% padding around the main graphic. This also serves a practical purpose: both iOS and Android apply corner masking to icons, and shapes that are too close to the edge can get clipped. App Icon Studio's padding slider makes this easy — start at 18px (the default) and adjust until the icon feels balanced and well-framed.

Quick checklist before you export: One clear focal point ✓ — Readable at 60px ✓ — Distinctive background color ✓ — No unnecessary text ✓ — Comfortable padding ✓

Putting it all together

Great icon design isn't about following rules rigidly — it's about communicating clearly and memorably within tight constraints. The constraints are small size, a split-second viewing time, and competition from every other app on the screen. Work within those constraints deliberately, and your icon will stand out.
